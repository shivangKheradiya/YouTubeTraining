# 🏷️ Identifier Names

## Overview
In C, **identifiers** are names given to variables, functions, labels, and other user-defined items. Choosing proper identifiers is essential for writing clear, maintainable, and portable code.  

Identifiers must follow specific rules regarding their composition, length, and uniqueness.

---

## ✅ Rules for Identifier Names
- The **first character** must be a **letter** (A–Z, a–z) or an **underscore** (`_`).  
- Subsequent characters may be **letters, digits, or underscores**.  
- Identifiers can be of **any length**, but only a certain number of characters are guaranteed to be significant depending on the C standard.  
- Identifiers are **case-sensitive**: `count`, `Count`, and `COUNT` are treated as three distinct names.  
- Identifiers **cannot**:
  - Be the same as a **C keyword** (e.g., `int`, `return`).  
  - Use names of **standard library functions** (e.g., `printf`, `malloc`).  

---

## ✔️ Examples
| Correct Identifiers | Incorrect Identifiers |
|---------------------|-----------------------|
| `count`             | `1count` (cannot start with digit) |
| `test23`            | `hi!there` (invalid character `!`) |
| `high_balance`      | `high...balance` (invalid characters `.`) |

---

## 🔗 External vs. Internal Identifiers
C distinguishes between two types of identifiers:

- **External Identifiers**  
  - Used in external linkage (e.g., function names, global variables shared across files).  
  - **C89:** At least the first 6 characters are significant.  
  - **C99:** At least the first 31 characters are significant.  

- **Internal Identifiers**  
  - Used locally (e.g., local variables inside functions).  
  - **C89:** At least the first 31 characters are significant.  
  - **C99:** At least the first 63 characters are significant.  

📌 **Note:** In C++, at least the first **1,024 characters** of an identifier are significant, which is far more generous than C standards.

---

## ⚖️ Significance of Identifier Length
- While identifiers may be long, compilers may only guarantee recognition of a certain number of leading characters.  
- This matters when converting programs between **C89 → C99** or **C → C++**, as identifiers that differ only after the significant length may be treated as identical in older standards.
